Mastering Pronunciation

The International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) is your secret weapon. It shows you exactly how a word sounds, removing the guesswork from reading a new language.

Click on a language below to show its specific phonetic rules:

Spanish Pronunciation Guide

Spanish is highly phonetic, meaning words are generally pronounced exactly as they are spelled. Here are the core sounds you need to master:

IPA Symbol English Equivalent Spanish Example
/a/ Like the 'a' in father casa (/ˈka.sa/)
/e/ Like the 'e' in bet perro (/ˈpe.ro/)
/ɲ/ Like the 'ny' in canyon (written as 'ñ') niño (/ˈni.ɲo/)
/x/ A raspy 'h', like the Scottish loch (written as 'j' or 'g') jabón (/xaˈβon/)

French Pronunciation Guide

French has many nasal vowels and silent letters at the end of words. Here are some of the most important sounds:

IPA Symbol English Equivalent French Example
/ʁ/ The famous "guttural R". Pronounced in the back of the throat. rouge (/ʁuʒ/)
/y/ Say 'ee' but round your lips tightly like 'oo'. tu (/ty/)
/õ/ A nasal 'o'. Say 'o' but push the air through your nose. bon (/bõ/)

German Pronunciation Guide

German pronunciation is quite strict. Once you learn the rules, you can pronounce almost any word.

IPA Symbol English Equivalent German Example
/ç/ A soft, hissing 'sh' sound made near the roof of the mouth (written as 'ch'). ich (/ɪç/)
/v/ Like the English 'v' (written as 'w'). Wasser (/ˈvasɐ/)
/ts/ Like the 'ts' in cats (written as 'z'). Zeit (/tsaɪ̯t/)
